---
title: Rainbow and Sequence
description: Complete a set of six and everyone involved discards.
---

A **Rainbow** or a **Sequence** is six cards in a row on the used pile. One player can make it alone, or several players can build it together, each on their own turn.

- On your turn, you play either **one card**, or **all the cards still needed** to complete it at once. Never just part of what's missing.
- Normal matching still applies to every card.
- Purple cards never count, except [Choose Color](#choose-color).

When it's complete, **everyone who contributed** discards. Each player discards the same amount, no matter how many cards they put in. Discarded cards go to the [graveyard](./piles.mdx#graveyard).

## Rainbow

The same card in all six colours: a number, or a **Block**, **Reverse**, **+2** or **+4**. Six **Choose Colors** also make one (see below). Everyone who contributed discards **1** card.

<Callout title="Example">
A yellow 1 is on the used pile. On your turn, you play the other five 1s. That's a Rainbow made by two players: the one who played the yellow 1 and you. Both of you discard 1.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Example">
1. A plays a yellow 1.
2. B plays a red 1.
3. You play the four 1s still missing.

All three of you discard 1. It also works if six players each play one 1: all six discard 1.
</Callout>

### Rainbow of action cards

Action cards still work normally while the Rainbow is being built.

#### Block

Each Block blocks the next player as usual.

<Callout title="Example">
1. P1 plays a red Block. P2 is blocked.
2. P3 plays a yellow Block. P4 is blocked.
</Callout>

When you play several Blocks at once, each one blocks a player: the next players, one per Block.

<Callout title="Example">
1. P1 plays a red Block. P2 is blocked.
2. You play the other five Blocks. The next five players are blocked.
</Callout>

<Callout title="Example">
Four Blocks of the Rainbow are already down. You play the last two. The next two players are blocked.
</Callout>

The blocks go around the table, so with more Blocks than other players, you can end up blocking yourself.

#### Reverse

Reverses work the same way, but when you play several at once, only an odd number of them reverses the direction. An even number cancels out, and the direction stays the same.

<Callout title="Example">
- You play three Reverses at once: the direction reverses.
- You play two Reverses at once: the direction stays the same.
</Callout>

#### +2 and +4

They [Chain](./chains.mdx) normally. Every +2 or +4 in the Rainbow adds to the Chain.

#### Choose Color

Choose Color only comes in purple, but it's the one purple card that can make a Rainbow. Six Choose Colors in a row make a Rainbow, whatever colour each of them chose.

## Sequence

Six consecutive numbers, going up or down. 0 counts, so a Sequence can be 0-1-2-3-4-5 up to 4-5-6-7-8-9, or the same backwards. Since every card has to match the one before it, a Sequence is always one colour. Everyone who contributed discards **2** cards.

<Callout title="Example">
1. A plays a green 4.
2. B plays a green 5.
3. You play green 6, 7, 8 and 9.

All three of you discard 2.
</Callout>

## Not allowed

You can't play only part of what's missing and leave the rest to the next player.

<Callout title="Example">
1. A yellow 1 is on the used pile.
2. You play four 1s.
3. The next player plays the last 1.

This isn't a Rainbow. On your turn, you either play all five, or just one.
</Callout>

## Discarding your last card

If the discard would leave you with no cards, you die. That includes completing a Rainbow or Sequence with your last card: you have nothing left to discard.

<Callout title="Strategy">
When a player is down to their last cards, the rest of the table can work together to complete a Rainbow or Sequence with them in it. The discard kills them.
</Callout>
